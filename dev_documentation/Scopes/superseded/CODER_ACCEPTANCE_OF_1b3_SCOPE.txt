And your understanding of the three text files is correct.

They are generated Zig 0.16.0 `builtin` captures showing what Zig actually assigned to each named CPU model on the Windows/MSVC target:

```text
std.Target.x86.cpu.x86_64
std.Target.x86.cpu.x86_64_v2
std.Target.x86.cpu.x86_64_v3
```

They are not themselves the formal x86-64 psABI specification. Each confirms Zig 0.16.0 and identifies the selected named model and its effective feature set.   

Their roles are different:

```text
Formal psABI standard and the approved scope:
    define which architectural features constitute v1, v2 and v3.

Intel/AMD architecture documentation:
    defines where those capabilities are detected through CPUID/XGETBV.

These three Zig captures:
    show what Zig 0.16.0 actually places in its named model feature sets.

The Stage 1B.3 comptime cross-check:
    reconciles the Zig sets with the formal membership through an explicit
    name-mapping and exclusion table.
```

The captures also confirm why that reconciliation is necessary. Zig mixes formal architectural features with compiler tuning and code-generation properties.

For example, the v1 capture includes the expected architectural entries such as `cmov`, `cx8`, `fxsr`, `mmx`, `sse`, `sse2`, and `x87`, but also entries such as:

```text
idivq_to_divl
macrofusion
nopl
slow_3ops_lea
slow_incdec
vzeroupper
```

Those are not separate psABI v1 membership rows. 

The v2 model adds the expected architectural features, plus tuning properties:

```text
Expected/mapped:
    cx16
    popcnt
    sahf
    sse3
    sse4_1
    sse4_2
    ssse3

Excluded or separately classified:
    crc32
    false_deps_popcnt
    fast_15bytenop
    fast_scalar_fsqrt
    fast_shld_rotate
    slow_unaligned_mem_32
```



The v3 model similarly contains the expected architectural additions:

```text
avx
avx2
bmi
bmi2
f16c
fma
lzcnt
movbe
```

but also contains tuning/code-generation properties and `xsave`, which is not the formal psABI `OSXSAVE` membership row:

```text
allow_light_256_bit
false_deps_lzcnt_tzcnt
fast_variable_crosslane_shuffle
fast_variable_perlane_shuffle
xsave
```



The initial complete reconciliation indicated by these captures is therefore:

```text
Name mappings:
    x87   <-> FPU
    cx16  <-> CMPXCHG16B
    sahf  <-> LAHF-SAHF
    bmi   <-> BMI1

Formal policy rows absent from Zig's model sets:
    OSFXSR
    SCE

Non-membership exclusions:
    64bit
    crc32
    xsave

Tuning/code-generation exclusions:
    allow_light_256_bit
    false_deps_lzcnt_tzcnt
    false_deps_popcnt
    fast_15bytenop
    fast_scalar_fsqrt
    fast_shld_rotate
    fast_variable_crosslane_shuffle
    fast_variable_perlane_shuffle
    idivq_to_divl
    macrofusion
    nopl
    slow_3ops_lea
    slow_incdec
    slow_unaligned_mem_32
    vzeroupper
```

The disappearing tuning entries between levels, such as `slow_incdec` disappearing at v2 and `slow_unaligned_mem_32` disappearing at v3, further demonstrate that the captures are complete compiler model descriptions rather than simple cumulative psABI membership lists.
