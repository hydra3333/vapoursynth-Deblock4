--- GAIS RESPONSE, VERBATIM ---

Yes, that statement is correct.
In MPEG-2 (ISO/IEC 13818-2), the Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) always
operates on blocks of **$8 \times 8$ samples**, regardless of whether the
plane is luma or chroma, and regardless of the chroma subsampling format
(4:2:0, 4:2:2, or 4:4:4).
The coordinates and dimensions of these blocks are defined relative to the
sampling grid of the specific plane being coded (the chroma plane's own
coordinates), not the luma plane's coordinates.
Here is how this relationship works across the different chroma formats
within a single macroblock (which always covers a $16 \times 16$ region of
luma pixels):

### 1. 4:2:0 Format
* **Luma ($Y$):** The $16 \times 16$ luma region is divided into four
  $8 \times 8$ luma blocks.
* **Chroma ($Cb, Cr$):** The chroma components are subsampled by a factor
  of 2 both horizontally and vertically. This means the $16 \times 16$ luma
  area corresponds to an $8 \times 8$ area of samples in each of the chroma
  planes.
* **DCT Blocks:** Each chroma plane ($Cb$ and $Cr$) contributes exactly
  **one** $8 \times 8$ block.
* *In luma coordinates*, this $8 \times 8$ chroma block spans a
  $16 \times 16$ pixel area, but the DCT is performed on the $8 \times 8$
  actual samples present in the chroma plane.

### 2. 4:2:2 Format
* **Luma ($Y$):** Four $8 \times 8$ blocks.
* **Chroma ($Cb, Cr$):** The chroma is subsampled by a factor of 2
  horizontally, but not vertically. The $16 \times 16$ luma area
  corresponds to an $8 \times 16$ area of samples in each chroma plane.
* **DCT Blocks:** Each chroma plane is divided vertically into **two**
  $8 \times 8$ blocks.
* *In luma coordinates*, each of these chroma DCT blocks covers an
  $8 \times 16$ area, but the data array processed by the DCT is still
  $8 \times 8$ samples in the chroma plane's own grid.

### 3. 4:4:4 Format
* **Luma ($Y$):** Four $8 \times 8$ blocks.
* **Chroma ($Cb, Cr$):** There is no subsampling. The $16 \times 16$ luma
  area corresponds to a $16 \times 16$ area of samples in each chroma
  plane.
* **DCT Blocks:** Each chroma plane is divided into **four** $8 \times 8$
  blocks.
* Here, the chroma plane coordinates match the luma coordinates exactly,
  and each of the four blocks per chroma channel is $8 \times 8$ samples.

---

### Summary of Block Counts per Macroblock
Because the DCT engine in MPEG-2 always expects an $8 \times 8$ matrix of
values, the number of coded blocks per macroblock changes depending on the
chroma format:

| Format | Luma Blocks ($8\times8$) | Chroma Blocks ($8\times8$) per MB | Total Blocks per MB |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **4:2:0** | 4 | 2 (1 $Cb$, 1 $Cr$) | 6 |
| **4:2:2** | 4 | 4 (2 $Cb$, 2 $Cr$) | 8 |
| **4:4:4** | 4 | 8 (4 $Cb$, 4 $Cr$) | 12 |

If the blocks were defined in luma coordinates, a 4:2:0 chroma block would
have to be processed as a $4 \times 4$ DCT, which the MPEG-2 specification
does not support. The hardware or software pipeline always processes
$8 \times 8$ matrices of samples directly from the respective coordinate
system of the plane being decoded.
